Does Wikipedia tell the same story everywhere?

A geographic bias analysis of 211 Wikipedia articles on buildings by 8 Italian architects — from Palladio to Renzo Piano.

1. The story

We scraped 211 Wikipedia pages — one for every building attributed to eight celebrated Italian architects. We measured each article's text length as a proxy for how much attention Wikipedia gives to a building.

Our central question:

Do buildings located abroad receive more detailed Wikipedia coverage than buildings in Italy — even though the architects are Italian?

The answer, supported by statistical testing, is a clear yes. Articles about buildings in the United States are roughly 4× longer than those in Italy. The effect holds across all non-Italian countries combined.

2. Audience

This visualization targets an academic audience — students and researchers interested in data bias, digital humanities, and computational approaches to architectural history. We assume comfort with basic statistical concepts (median, IQR, skewness) and aim for explanatory rather than exploratory communication.

4. Evaluation and reflection

What the visualization confirms

The geographic bias is clear and statistically significant. Buildings located in English-speaking countries (US, UK) receive substantially more detailed Wikipedia coverage. This likely reflects the demographics of English Wikipedia's editor base rather than any inherent difference in architectural significance.

Unexpected findings

Italy's extreme kurtosis (68.07) was the most surprising discovery. While Italy's median article length is modest (2,484 chars), a single entry — St. Peter's Basilica at 56,561 characters — creates an extreme outlier that inflates the mean and produces an extraordinarily heavy-tailed distribution. The Architectural Chernoff skyline makes this visible at a glance through Italy's disproportionately tall chimney.

Carlo Maderno's paradox: Despite having only 14 buildings (the fewest), his articles average 10,587 characters — the highest per-building coverage of any architect. This is because several of his works (St. Peter's Basilica, Palazzo Barberini) are globally iconic landmarks that attract extensive Wikipedia editing.

Small-sample countries like Japan (n=2, median 10,433) and Malta (n=2, median 8,188) show high medians, but their tiny sample sizes mean these figures are unreliable. The Architectural Chernoff makes this limitation visible: their buildings look dramatic but lack the "texture" (floor count, window variation) that comes from larger samples.

Limitations

Text length is a coarse proxy for coverage quality — a long article may be verbose rather than informative. Future work could incorporate revision count, reference count, or readability scores. Additionally, 2 of 211 records (~1%) could not be country-matched automatically and were excluded from analysis.
